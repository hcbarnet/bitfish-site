Ten waters. 120 fish.

A pixel fishing game about working one stretch of water until you know it, then earning the next.

Start on a farm pond with a worm and a wooden rod. Log enough of what lives there and Cedar Creek opens, then Granite Lake, and on through the bog, the river, the pier and the marsh until you are standing on a charter deck over the drop-off with the best line money can buy.

What is in it

  • 120 species across ten waters, each with its own habits — what it eats, when it feeds, how deep it holds.
  • A real rig system. A lure fishes on its own; bait needs a hook. Fish refuse the wrong thing.
  • Fights you can lose. Hold too hard and the line wears through. It never mends.
  • A day that turns. Dawn, day, dusk and night, and plenty that only moves in the dark.
  • People who know things. Thirty anglers on the banks, and what they tell you is true.

Playing it quietly

Bitfish has no account, no server and no analytics. Your progress lives on your device and nowhere else. The only thing that reaches the internet is an advertisement, and those are configured child-directed and non-personalised whoever is playing.

A one-time purchase removes them entirely, and an optional PIN keeps the coin store shut.
